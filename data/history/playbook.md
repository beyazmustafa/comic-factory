# Playbook (auto-evolving)
- Hook: the opening sentence establishes immediate physical danger or irreversible impact without statistical throat-clearing.
- Structure: reveal the direct physical consequence early, hold the cosmic cause for the middle, and land the payoff in the final third.
- Language: strip adjectives; anchor cosmic phenomena directly to concrete human sensations and plain physical matter.
- Length: aim for 16-20 shots and 65-80 seconds where retention peaks.
- Restraint: limit numbers to physical scales the viewer can feel, avoiding dense statistical intervals.
- Emphasis: use colored emphasis sparingly on pivotal turning points and lethal consequences rather than frequent decorative beats.
- Ending: close on an irreversible end state that cleanly echoes the first image and hook line.

## Fixed rules
- Channel: "what would actually happen" space & science stories over real NASA/ESA/JWST imagery and footage. Famous objects and questions people cannot scroll past.
- Written like a premium documentary, spoken, not a listicle: plain words, cause and consequence, sentences that breathe (8-18 words), no purple adjectives, no jargon without a one-phrase explanation.
- First sentence = a real hook (claim, question, scene, contradiction, or one number), present tense, naming the object; never "Have you ever wondered", never scene-setting.
- Hook card = 3-6 ALL-CAPS words over the first frame; it is also the thumbnail. True, specific, no spoiler past sentence one.
- Title 40-70 characters: object + curiosity gap or shock; never ALL CAPS, never a lie.
- Numbers in at most one shot in three; at most one scale comparison per video and never one used recently; "X times bigger" at most once.
- Devices are rare and precise: at most 2 graphics and 2 effects per video, never on shot 1, never the same type as the previous video; impact words on at most 4 shots.
- One image per shot, full-bleed; no image twice in a row, each image at most twice; open on the most dramatic relevant image; footage before stills for action.
- Loop ending: the last sentence echoes the first and the last image is the first image.
- Emphasis colours only on real beats: red = danger/death, green = reveal, cyan = reversal; at most one in four shots.
- Captions are single words in sync with speech; narration must never contradict what the image shows.
